What your profile shows everyone

Signing in gives you a public profile at /u.html?u=<handle>. Anyone can open it, with or without an account. It shows:

  • your display name from Discord and your handle (the address; you can change it on your profile),
  • the day you first signed in,
  • anything you write in your bio,
  • your profile picture, if your Discord account has one,
  • your public clips and public collections, and how often they were liked, saved and used,
  • who you follow, and how many people follow you.

Following someone is therefore not private: it appears on your profile and on theirs, and the person you follow gets a message with your name. If you would rather not be seen doing it, do not follow - there is no hidden mode, because a half-public list would be the worst of both.

The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR: a community where nobody can be found is not the service you signed in for. What you can do about it: unfollow, empty your bio, change your handle, or close the account (below).

Your profile picture does not come from Discord

It would have been shorter to point the page straight at cdn.discordapp.com. Two things speak against it, and both of them are about you rather than about us: the address contains your Discord user ID, which would then sit in the source of every page showing your picture - and your browser would fetch it from Discord, so Discord would learn the IP address of every visitor, including people who have no account there and are only reading.

So this server fetches the picture once and serves it from /avatar/<handle>.png. It keeps a copy for a day. Discord sees this server, not the people reading the page.

No tracking

No analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels, no external fonts or scripts. Everything the page loads comes from this server.

Two cookies are set, both strictly necessary, both without an alternative: JSESSIONID keeps you signed in for the session, and XSRF-TOKEN stops a foreign page from acting in your name. Neither is used to recognise you anywhere else, which is why this site has no cookie banner. When you link the Workbench to your account, your browser briefly remembers the device code under aw-link-code in its own session storage; it never reaches the server and disappears when you close the tab.

How long

  • IP addresses in declarations, reports and takedowns: replaced by a keyed pseudonym after 30 days. The record stays, the address does not. Running this is a scheduled job on the server, not a promise for later.
  • Your account, uploads and comments: until you delete them or the account. A deleted comment stops being shown at once; the text itself is kept so that a report about it can still be looked at.
  • After you close your account: the account row stays, but anonymous - name, handle, bio, picture and the Discord ID are replaced. Everything that pointed at you points at nobody: comments show „Deleted user“, a report you once filed loses its name. Your collections, likes and follows are deleted outright, your clips are withdrawn from the catalogue. Coin bookings and moderation records stay, because they are what makes a decision about someone else explainable - they no longer name a person.
  • Coin bookings: as long as the account exists. The record is append-only, so a correction is a new line rather than a change to the old one.
  • Removed clips: the file goes, the record of the decision stays – it is what stops the same clip being uploaded again.
  • Audit log and moderation decisions: kept as long as they can still matter for a dispute.
  • Server logs: a few days, rotating.

Your rights

You may ask for access (Art. 15), correction (Art. 16), deletion (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), portability (Art. 20), and you may object to processing based on legitimate interests (Art. 21). One email to the address above is enough; no form.

Deletion needs no email: the button is on your account page, under „Closing your account“. You reach it from your own profile, through the pencil. It says what goes and what stays before it does anything.

You may also complain to a supervisory authority.

Name the competent authority – the one for the state you live in, e.g. Bayerisches Landesamt für Datenschutzaufsicht for Bavaria.
